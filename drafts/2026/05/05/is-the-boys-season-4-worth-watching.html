Is The Boys Season 4 Worth Watching? A Fan’s Verdict

Why Season 4 Starts with a Bang

If you’re asking “is The Boys season 4 worth watching,” the answer starts the moment the opening credits roll. Episode 1, “The Only Man In The Sky,” drops us straight into a Vought‑run crisis that forces Billy Butcher (Karl Urban) and Hughie (Jack Quaid) to make a split‑second decision that reshapes the entire boardroom power play.

Unlike the slower burn of season 3’s mid‑season arc, season 4 launches with a high‑octane chase through a downtown rooftop, a surprise cameo by Soldier Boy (Jensen Ackles), and a shocking reveal that Homelander (Antony Starr) has been secretly funding a new “clean‑energy” program. The pacing feels relentless, and that’s exactly why fans who crave adrenaline can’t wait to binge.

The New Villains and Old Enemies

Season 4 introduces a fresh threat in the form of the “Tempest” syndicate, a covert group that supplies super‑powered mercenaries to the highest bidder. Their leader, Dr. Mina (Jodie Comer), is a genius biotech engineer who can reprogram Compound V on the fly. She first appears in episode 3, “The Man Who Got Away,” where she hijacks a Vought transport and leaves a trail of altered supers.

At the same time, familiar faces return with new motives. Stormfront (Aya Cash) makes a brief but pivotal appearance in episode 5, “The End of the World,” where she allies with Tempest to sabotage Vought’s PR campaign. Queen Maeve (Dominique McElligott) finally confronts her own guilt in episode 6, “The Last One,” taking a stand against both Homelander and the syndicate.

Character Arcs That Keep You Hooked

Butcher’s journey is the most volatile. After learning that his son, Ryan, survived the Compound V experiment, he swings between ruthless vengeance and desperate protection. Episode 4, “The Good, The Bad, and The Ugly,” shows him teaming up with a reluctant Starlight (Erin Moriarty) to infiltrate a Vought research lab, resulting in a heart‑wrenching sacrifice that redefines his moral line.

Hughie finally gets his moment of agency. In episode 7, “The Little Things,” he uncovers a hidden Vought ledger that exposes the extent of the corporation’s lies. This discovery pushes him to confront his own complicity, culminating in a quiet but powerful monologue that resonates long after the credits.

Starlight’s arc shines brightest. She balances her public hero persona with a secret rebellion that culminates in episode 8, “The Darkest Night.” Her decision to broadcast the truth about Tempest’s experiments on live TV is a game‑changer, and the scene where she stands beside Butcher on the rooftop—lights flickering behind them—has become an instant fan favorite.

  • Homelander: more unhinged than ever, using a new “solar‑powered” ability introduced in episode 2.
  • Mother’s Milk: provides the emotional anchor, especially in episode 9, “The Long Road Home,” where he rescues a group of kidnapped children.
  • Frenchie: returns with a dark past, revealed through a flashback in episode 10, “The Last Stand.”

What the Finale Means for Future Seasons

The season finale, “The End Is Near,” ties together the chaos with a cliffhanger that leaves Vought’s leadership in ruins and Homelander isolated on a floating platform above the city. The final shot—Butcher staring at a broken Vought logo while the sun rises—asks the same question fans are asking now: can the series sustain its momentum?

In short, season 4 delivers the gritty violence, dark humor, and moral ambiguity that made the first three seasons a cultural phenomenon. If you love twisted superhero drama, the answer to “is The Boys season 4 worth watching” is a resounding yes. It’s a roller‑coaster you’ll want to ride again and again.
