Game of Thrones Ending Explained: What Really Happened?

The Shock Reveal: Bran Stark as King

When the council in the throne room finally votes, the name that drops like a bomb is Bran Stark. After a season of dragon fire and political chaos, the audience is left wondering why a crippled boy with a mysterious past gets the Iron Throne. The writers point to his ability to see the past and future, arguing that a ruler who can remember the kingdom’s mistakes is the safest bet. Episode 6, "The Iron Throne," frames his coronation with a quiet, almost eerie ceremony that feels more like a promise than a celebration.

Fans immediately started dissecting the "Three-Eyed Raven" angle. Was Bran’s detachment a narrative shortcut, or a clever way to avoid a power vacuum? The show leans into his aloofness—he barely reacts to the applause, hinting that the throne is now a symbolic seat rather than a source of personal ambition.

  • Key moment: Samwell’s suggestion that a wise ruler should be chosen, not a warrior.
  • Character impact: Sansa’s disappointment fuels her next move toward an independent North.

Jon Snow’s Bitter Choice: Returning to the Night’s Watch

Jon’s fate feels like a cruel twist of fate after surviving countless battles. The moment he slays Daenerys, his heart shatters, and the council decides he must be sent back to the Night’s Watch—a punishment that feels more like exile than justice. Episode 6 shows Jon walking away from the newly crowned Bran, his expression a mix of grief and resignation.

Why send him north when the Wall is technically defunct? The answer lies in the symbolic nature of the Night’s Watch: a place for those who have broken oaths to atone. By returning Jon to the far north, the series closes his arc with a bittersweet nod to his original destiny.

  • Fan theory: Jon’s true parentage (Rhaegar + Lyanna) makes his exile a way to keep Targaryen blood out of power.
  • Emotional beat: The final shot of Jon leading the Free Folk beyond the Wall hints at a new, hopeful chapter.

The Fate of Daenerys: From Dragon Queen to Tragic End

Daenerys Targaryen’s descent into madness culminates in the iconic hallway scene where she decides the lives of thousands over a single throne. The camera lingers on her face as she looks at the charred bodies, and the audience is forced to ask: was this always inevitable? Episode 5, "The Bells," pushes her over the edge, turning a beloved liberator into a tyrant in minutes.

The aftermath is swift—her death at the hands of Jon feels both tragic and necessary. The show frames it as a mercy killing, a moment where love and duty clash. This act also resolves the long‑standing question of whether power corrupts absolutely, even someone who started with pure intentions.

  • Key scene: Daenerys’s last look at the ruined King’s Landing, mirroring her earlier hopeful vision of a free world.
  • Character resolution: Tyrion’s broken heart and subsequent decision to advocate for Bran’s rule.

Loose Ends and Fan Theories: What Still Matters

Even after the credits roll, fans are left with lingering questions. What happens to the dragons? The show hints they’re still alive—one perishes in the sea, another roosts on the distant island of Dragonstone’s ruins, and the third, Drogon, flies away, leaving his ultimate fate to imagination.

Sansa’s proclamation of Northern independence satisfies a long‑running storyline, yet it also raises diplomatic questions. Will the North remain isolated, or will trade and alliances reshape the continent?

Meanwhile, Arya’s decision to explore west of Westeros opens a whole new map for future tales. Her farewell to the audience is a promise that the world is bigger than the Seven Kingdoms.

  • Speculation: Bran’s visions could foreshadow a sequel series focusing on the mystical beyond‑the‑Wall realms.
  • Fan favorite: The ambiguous smile of the Night King’s empty throne, hinting at possible spin‑offs.
